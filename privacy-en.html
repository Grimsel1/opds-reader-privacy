Developer and contact

OPDS Reader is developed by Sergey Tolstolutskiy (@Grimsel). This policy covers the Google Play version (package ru.grimsel.opdsreader.play). For questions about data or the app, contact Stolstoluckii@gmail.com. The app reads your files and the OPDS catalogs you connect. It does not use an OPDS Reader account or a developer-operated library server.

Data on your device

The app stores added books, file and selected-folder paths, covers, descriptions, authors and series, search queries, catalog addresses, subscriptions, reading positions, bookmarks, quotes, notes, reading statistics, settings, models and temporary files on your device. These support the library, reading, search and notifications. Scanning processes file contents locally. The app does not request access to contacts, location, camera or microphone. The app's own code contains no advertising or separate developer analytics service. The third-party translation SDK has diagnostics, described below. Your interface language preference is stored locally; it does not change your books or their language metadata.

File access and background tasks

Open files with Android's document picker or grant access to selected folders. The Google Play version does not request access to all files: it scans only folders you select in Android's picker. You can add multiple folders; access is retained until you remove a folder or revoke its permission in Android. Notification permission is used for new books and ongoing tasks. User-started scans and long operations show a progress notification. Automatic folder and metadata checks use ordinary WorkManager tasks without starting a foreground service. Read-aloud uses a media playback service and notification. Android schedules subscription checks; system restrictions and network availability may delay them. Permissions can be revoked in Android settings. Choose a catalog download folder with Android's picker. The permission is kept on the device, and an internal working copy is retained for offline reading. Files in your chosen download folder are not removed when you delete a library entry or uninstall the app; manage them separately. External file providers process writes under their own policies.

Catalogs and internet downloads

Browsing, searching, downloading covers or books, and checking subscriptions contact your selected catalog and resource addresses supplied by it. The server receives your IP address, request parameters, requested-resource details and technical HTTP headers. Enabled prefetching may request the next page in advance. Restricted catalogs receive the credentials or tokens you provide. Credentials are protected using Android Keystore. The catalog operator determines server-side retention. Public catalogs without sign-in may use HTTP; those addresses and request parameters are not encrypted. Authentication requires HTTPS: usernames, passwords, tokens and authenticated cookies are not sent over HTTP, including after redirects. Do not put secrets in catalog addresses. The app developer does not control third-party catalog content or availability. Catalog session cookies are stored encrypted on your device to maintain sign-in for downloads, redirects, covers and background checks. Cookie transmission is restricted by the server's scope, secure transport and port; signing out removes the session. Passwords, tokens and saved account sessions are excluded from library backups.

Cloud sync and note exports

Only after you connect Mail.ru Cloud does the app transfer books, covers, reading positions, bookmarks, quotes, collections, subscriptions, profiles and other sync data to your chosen cloud account. Sign-in uses your email and app password, protected using Android Keystore. You may choose to send quotes and notes to Readwise, Joplin or Notion using an access token. These services have their own terms and retention policies. Disconnecting sync or an integration stops future transfers but does not delete data already stored by that service.

Speech, dictionaries and translation

System text-to-speech supplies text to your selected Android speech engine. The engine may run locally or online according to its own settings and policy. Supertonic generates speech on your device after its model is downloaded from Hugging Face. Silero Russian speech and contextual word stress also run on your device. Its optional model files are downloaded from Silero (models.silero.ai) and the Python Package Index file CDN (files.pythonhosted.org). Book text is not sent to those servers; the app only reads verified numerical model data from the archives. Models, dictionaries, morphology databases and extra fonts are downloaded at your request; their servers receive technical request data. Offline dictionaries and OCR process content on the device. Wiktionary lookup sends the selected word to Wikimedia. Online translation opens Google Translate and sends the selected text to Google. ML Kit translation runs on the device; that module does not send the text or translation result to Google. However, ML Kit uses Google/Firebase for model downloads, configuration and diagnostics, transmitting device and app version information, installation identifiers, performance measurements, errors and selected languages. The app therefore does not claim that third-party SDKs collect no data. DeepL is connected separately: at your request, selected text is sent to DeepL. It requires your own API key; DeepL's plan and policies govern processing. A saved key is protected using Android Keystore and excluded from backups. The Google Play version contains no RuStore update SDK. Its update button opens the app's Google Play page without sending your library or notes to the developer.

Transfers you initiate

Sharing sends selected books, links or information to the app you choose in Android's share sheet. Backups and exports are created in your chosen location and may contain personal notes and library content. Recipients process them under their own policies. You start Calibre Wireless to transfer books from a selected computer. During that session, the computer receives a random app instance identifier for the Calibre protocol and details of linked books, including file names and metadata. In the Google Play version, phone-to-computer connections use TLS 1.2 or later. Certificates are verified using Android's trust store and server hostname, or a SHA-256 fingerprint from an imported connection file with a validity-period check. Standard Calibre Wireless requires a TLS gateway on your computer; an unencrypted segment is permitted only over that computer's loopback interface. The gateway key is checked after TLS is established and before Calibre is contacted. Unencrypted connections and automatic fallback to them are disabled in the Play version. A connection file contains an access key: transfer it privately and do not publish it. Stop the session in the app or its notification. The identifier is generated by the app and is not an IMEI, Android ID or MAC address. Play connections are restricted to local and private IP addresses; public internet addresses are blocked. Connection instructions and data disclosures appear before connecting. After five minutes without book or metadata exchange, the session ends automatically; this timer does not interrupt a book transfer in progress.

Retention, deletion and protection

Local data remains until you no longer need it or delete it. Delete books and entries in the library, clear temporary files using the app's tools, or remove all internal data using Clear storage or Uninstall in Android. Original files in shared folders, your backups and cloud data are not automatically removed when you uninstall the app; manage them separately. Data held by catalogs, Google, Wikimedia, Mail.ru and connected services is deleted under the recipient's policies. Passwords and access tokens protected using Android Keystore are excluded from ordinary library backups. Catalog addresses and user-written entries are included: do not place secrets in names, notes or addresses you share. Book files, notes and exports do not have the app's own end-to-end encryption; consider who can access your device and chosen cloud storage.

Search and library migration

Local full-text and vector indexes may contain book excerpts and numerical representations of text. They remain in private app storage and are excluded from library backups. Ordinary indexing and search do not send text over the network. Importing a ReadEra, Moon+, KOReader or FBReader export processes the selected file locally and shows a preview before adding annotations or positions. Available information depends on the export format. Paths inside an export never grant access to other files.

Custom models and providers

Only after you choose a provider and explicitly consent does the app send selected text, a query, alignment excerpts, questions or selected books for embedding to your chosen compatible API. The screen identifies the recipient and data being sent. Requests may contain personal information from books or notes. Your chosen server operator determines retention, prices, quality and data use. The developer provides no built-in AI server. HTTPS is required; computer-hosted models connect through a TLS gateway. Certificate fingerprints verify the selected server, and access keys are protected using Android Keystore. Provider keys and configuration are excluded from backups and sync. Embedding and reading-guide jobs are started by the user, run in batches and can be stopped in the app or notification; completed results may remain on the device. Removing a provider stops future requests but does not delete information already held by its operator. Answers and guides remain local; saved notes and guides may synchronize when the notes category is enabled. A matching quotation can still accompany an incorrect interpretation; limiting sources to read text does not guarantee the absence of spoilers.

Audiobooks and sync categories

You choose MP3, M4B and other audio documents through the Android system picker. Device-specific file bindings, access permissions and playback are local. The microphone is not used. EPUB files may contain audio, video and SMIL timing; book scripts do not run. Track names, chapter timing and audio positions are reading data and may travel with a library backup or sync. Separately selected audio files and their access permissions are not synchronized; select the files again on another device. Sync categories control reading positions and history, notes, appearance, dictionaries, catalogs, statistics, the speech queue and selected book files. Turning a category off does not delete existing cloud data. Background playback displays a notification with pause and stop controls.

Study cards, PDF forms and reading services

Study cards store your questions, answers, tags, source-book references and review dates on the device. They are included in your library backup and in the notes category of optional cloud synchronization. Anki export writes only the cards you choose to a local TSV file. Opening a source link returns to the corresponding book on this device.

PDF form filling is processed locally. Draft field values are stored in private application storage and are not included in portable backups or cloud sync. Saving a filled copy writes the field values to the PDF file you select. PDF documents and field contents are not sent to the developer or to a form-processing server.

Hardcover is an optional service you connect using your own personal API token. Connection checks send the token to api.hardcover.app over HTTPS. Search sends the entered query. Loading your service library retrieves its book records. A confirmed send can transmit the matched book/edition identifier, reading status and progress; rating and review are transmitted only when explicitly selected. The full book, study cards, private annotations and local file paths are not uploaded through this connection. The token is encrypted on the device and excluded from backups and sync. Disconnecting removes the local token; previously sent records can be managed in your Hardcover account. Hardcover processes information under its own privacy policy.

StoryGraph/Goodreads exchange is a manual CSV import/export. The reader writes or reads the file you choose; it does not sign in to StoryGraph or automatically transmit this file. A CSV export can contain book titles, authors, reading status, collections, ratings and reviews. You control where you store or upload the resulting file.
